Care Pathway Overview

This carepath describes the home follow-up of lung transplant recipients. The main purpose is the early detection of a decline in graft function. That decline can be caused by acute rejection, infection or the onset of chronic lung allograft dysfunction (CLAD), the main limit on long-term survival after lung transplantation. Home spirometry can detect a decline in FEV1 earlier than the scheduled outpatient pulmonary function tests.

The other parameters complete the picture:

  • Oxygen saturation and symptoms: desaturation, dyspnea, cough or reduced exercise tolerance can accompany a fall in FEV1, and help distinguish a real decline from a poorly performed measurement.
  • Body temperature: lung transplant recipients are immunosuppressed and at high risk of respiratory infections. Fever is an early sign of infection, and infection itself is a risk factor for rejection.
  • Blood pressure: calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, ciclosporin) often cause hypertension and nephrotoxicity.
  • Body weight: reflects nutritional status, corticosteroid side effects and fluid balance.

  • Gastro-intestinal and neurological symptoms: diarrhea and vomiting can be caused by infection or medication, and change immunosuppressant blood levels. Tremor is a typical sign of calcineurin inhibitor toxicity.

  • Monitoring Duration: Long-term, starting after discharge following transplantation. Follow-up is most intensive in the first year.
  • Central Care Facility: Lung transplant centre with a dedicated follow-up team.
  • Data Source: A home spirometer, a pulse oximeter, a thermometer, a blood pressure monitor and a scale (connected or read manually), and a symptom questionnaire in the patient application.

Patient Eligibility

  • Indication: Recipient of a single or double lung transplant.
  • Consent: Signed informed consent for remote follow-up prior to enrolment.
  • Training: The patient is trained in performing forced expiratory manoeuvres before the start of home monitoring.

Process and Workflow Description

Start-Up

  • Informed Consent: Collected and securely stored.
  • Device Delivery and Training: The patient receives the home spirometer and is trained by the transplant team.
  • Parameters Monitored:
    • Spirometry (FEV1, and depending on the device FVC, FEV1/FVC, PEF and FEF25-75)
    • Oxygen Saturation (SpO2)
    • Body Temperature
    • Blood Pressure (BP)
    • Body Weight (BW)
    • Symptoms
  • Frequency: Defined by the transplant centre. Typically daily and at a fixed time of day, because lung function varies over the day. Symptoms are also registered whenever they occur.

Data Collection

  • Spirometry is performed by the patient and processed by the spirometer or its application. The application selects the reported values from the session and grades the session quality according to the ATS/ERS 2019 standard (see below).
  • Oxygen saturation, body temperature, blood pressure and body weight are collected automatically via connected devices or entered manually.
  • Symptoms are registered by the patient.
  • Technology is CE-certified, with end-to-end encryption and GDPR compliance.

Alarm Management

  • Baseline: Each patient’s own post-transplant baseline FEV1 is the reference, not the predicted value. Following the ISHLT 2019 CLAD consensus, the baseline is the mean of the two best post-operative FEV1 values measured at least 3 weeks apart. Predicted values are of limited use after transplantation, because the lungs come from a donor of a different size, age and sex.
  • Thresholds: Defined by the transplant centre. A typical home monitoring trigger is a sustained FEV1 decline of 10% or more from baseline. CLAD itself is defined as a persistent decline of 20% or more.
  • Quality: The FEV1 grade tells how far the value can be trusted. Grades A, B and C are reliable, grades D, E and U are interpreted with caution, and grade F is not used. A poorly performed manoeuvre usually underestimates FEV1, so a low value with a poor grade leads to a repeat measurement. It is not ignored: a decline that persists when the measurement is repeated is handled as a real decline.
  • Oxygen Saturation: A fall in SpO2 from the patient’s usual value should be assessed together with the FEV1 and the symptoms.
  • Body Temperature: Fever (typically 38 °C or above) warrants contact with the transplant team, as it may indicate infection.
  • Symptoms: New or worsening respiratory symptoms, and hemoptysis in particular, warrant contact with the transplant team, regardless of the spirometry result.
  • Blood Pressure and Weight: Thresholds defined by the transplant centre.
  • Review: Data is reviewed by the transplant team during working hours.

Spirometry

One Observation per session. A spirometry session is one sitting in which the patient performs several forced manoeuvres, typically 3 to 8. One Spirometry Observation is sent per session, not per manoeuvre. This follows the ATS/ERS 2019 reporting rules:

  • The reported FEV1 is the largest FEV1 of the acceptable manoeuvres, and the reported FVC is the largest FVC of the acceptable manoeuvres. They may come from different manoeuvres.
  • FEV1/FVC is calculated from the reported FEV1 and FVC.
  • FEF25-75 comes from the acceptable manoeuvre with the largest sum of FEV1 and FVC.
  • If no manoeuvre is acceptable, the values of the best usable manoeuvre are reported, with grade U.

The spirometer or its application selects the values. The receiving system therefore does not need to apply the ATS/ERS selection rules itself, and gets one value per session to compare against the patient’s baseline. Individual manoeuvres and curves are not exchanged as structured data; the curves are part of the PDF outcome module.

  • coding.display holds the official display name of the code system. A short label for clinicians may be given in code.text.
  • FEV1 is the only mandatory value, so handheld FEV1/PEF meters that do not measure FVC can also be used.
  • FEV1/FVC, FEF25-75, FVC % predicted and the FVC grade are only allowed when FVC is reported.
  • The units are fixed. A device that reports PEF in L/min converts it to L/s.
  • When predicted values are sent, the reference equation used (for example GLI-2012 or GLI Global 2022) should be stated in Observation.method.text.

The grades are the session-level grades of Table 10 of the ATS/ERS 2019 standard, and FEV1 and FVC are graded independently. The FEV1 grade SHOULD always be sent, and the FVC grade SHOULD be sent whenever FVC is sent. Remarks on the session, such as a reason for poor quality reported by the patient, go in Observation.note.

LOINC for the measurements. SNOMED CT is the first choice in Belgium (see Terminologies). The spirometry values are an exception, like the vital signs: LOINC is what spirometers and their applications produce, so the LOINC coding is required and the SNOMED CT code may be added next to it.

Local codes for the grades. SNOMED CT has no codes for the ATS/ERS 2019 session grades. LOINC has 64593-7 FEV1 quality attribute [PhenX] and 64592-9 FVC quality attribute [PhenX], but these belong to the PhenX research protocol, are in trial status, and their answer list (A to F) has no grade U. This IG therefore defines its own codes. A sender may add the LOINC PhenX code as a second coding on the grade component.
